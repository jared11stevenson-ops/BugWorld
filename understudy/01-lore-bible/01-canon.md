# UNDERSTUDY — Canon (the Truth)

> **SPOILERS. This is the only document that says everything.** Nothing here is ever told to the player in this form. The game exposes it as fragments, contradictions and tells (see `05-crossrefs-and-layers.md`).
> Sentences marked **[HARD]** are fixed canon and must never be contradicted by later writing. Sentences marked **[SOFT]** are the official answer, but the game may leave them unproven.

---

## 0. The truth in one page

Little Mercy sits on a ridge above a reservoir. Under the reservoir is the town that used to be there. In 1961 the dam closed early and the valley below the ridge drowned in minutes. The survivors, standing on the ridge, watched the lights go under. At dawn their pastor knelt on the new shoreline and prayed. *"Give me back my girl, and let nothing else be taken. Let everything stay as it is."*

The lake did what the words said. It is not a god or a monster. **It is a promise with water in it**, and it keeps promises literally.

His daughter walked up the shore, dry. The town has stayed exactly as it is ever since.

"Staying" has a mechanism. When someone in Little Mercy decides to change their life, the lake takes them in the quiet minute at 4:12 a.m. A replacement stands up in their bed the next morning, built out of the memory of someone who drowned in 1961. The town does not notice, and the lake makes sure of that. These replacements are **understudies**. The pastor's daughter was the first.

The player is the lake's other, unplanned kind of understudy. It is not one drowned person but what is left of all the rest, **a chorus of 149 memories** that woke up this August because the next one in the queue, a nine-year-old called Wren, refused to go. A chorus has no life of its own. Each morning it wears the life the lake has just emptied, and each evening it leaves behind a piece of itself to carry on in that life. So whatever the player does in a role becomes that person's permanent personality. By the last day the player is whatever is left over.

Every custom in town, including no goodbyes, waving at the lake, clocks that never change and the sign that says POP. 913, turns out to be grief in a costume.

**Theme in a sentence:** *staying is not the same as keeping.*

## 2. The rules of the lake [HARD unless marked]

**R1. The lake holds.** *Reservoir* means "a thing that keeps". The lake keeps memory, voices and habits of everyone who drowned. Their bodies lie in Low Mercy, which still stands under about 40 feet of water. The church spire is the only part within reach of a boat.

**R2. The Wish is literal.** The lake does exactly what the words said and no more. There is no malice in it. The villain of this story is a sentence.

**R3. A Vacancy is an empty place in town.** The Wish forbids empty places. A vacancy is created when a person is Taken, or when a walk-on "closes" (dies).

**R4. A Decision** is an irrevocable commitment to a different shape of life, made within earshot of the lake (roughly the valley rim) and put into the world: spoken, signed, sent, or written down with intent. Thinking about it does not count. Deciding does. *This is the mechanical definition behind "everyone who vanishes had just decided to change their life."* **The lake does not punish knowledge.** Vern could have aired Hymn #212 every night for a year. What got him Taken was retiring. Every one of the thirteen *changed the shape of their life*; several planned to expose something as part of it, and fans will assume exposure is the trigger. That wrong theory is deliberate and the game never confirms or denies it outright. The lake only cares what you are going to do, not what you know.

**R5. The Take.** At the next Slack (4:12 a.m.) the lake takes the Decider. Nobody sees it. There is a sound like a church bell heard through a wall.

**R6. The Still.** The Taken are not dead. They are kept alive and unaging in the drowned town, dreaming the last day they were free to change. They return if the hold breaks (Endings 2 and 4). Postcards from the Still arrive at the post office (see Connie, `03-cast-anchors.md`). **[SOFT: whether they are happy there.]**

**R7. The Hush.** The lake dims noticing. People accept the replacement as the person. Paperwork, phone calls and photographs smooth over. The Hush is strongest where the town agrees, and weaker in:
- children under about ten
- the very old (Harlan, Ottoline)
- anyone awake at 4:12
- anything written down in advance (notebooks, diaries, receipts)
- fog. When the town is afraid the Hush thins and the weather says so.

The Hush fades at the rim. This is why outsiders never linger and never ask twice.

**R8. The Slack.** For 60 seconds at 4:12 a.m. the Town Hall clock holds. In that minute the Hush loosens, the lake is audible, and understudies show their tells in still water. *(Night gameplay clock.)*

**R9. Walk-ons** (settled understudies). A walk-on is one drowned person's memory inside the Taken's body, plus the town's idea of the Taken. They believe they are the Taken until **Awakened**. They age with the body they were given, so a walk-on made from a 61-year-old memory in a 22-year-old body in 1962 is 84 today and has lived two lifetimes. They live ordinary lives and are sometimes happy. When they die they die in bed with lungs full of lake water. The Prompt Book calls this **"closed"**, and every walk-on who closed looked relieved. **[SOFT: closing is release, not reabsorption.]**

**R10. Awakening.** A walk-on wakes by seeing their own wrinkled fingertips in the Slack, by hearing Hymn #212 in full, or by hearing their Source's name said to them as a name and not an insult. Awakened walk-ons may go back to sleep. Not all do. A walk-on who Awakens and then *acts* (a Decision of their own) is **Struck**: dissolved to a damp outline, memory spent, not replaced.

**R11. The Rim.** Walk-ons beyond the valley rim "dry": the lent memory goes first, then the person. This is why none has ever left.

**R12. The Thin Weeks.** The Wish has a yearly term and the Raising renews it. In the three weeks before Founders' Day (about 25 Aug – 9 Sept) the lake is *thin*: people feel restless, Decisions come easily, Takes cluster. Of the 62 Takings since 1962, **every one** fell in this window. This season's 13 consecutive nights is not normal. Bea says so out loud on Day 9.

**R13. The Chorus ("the Swing").** The Mercy Players, who borrowed their theatre words from the Playhouse, call it **the Swing**, meaning the understudy who covers many roles. The Held, and Wren, call it **the Chorus**. All Held memories not yet spent act together as one, with no body of their own. Each Take empties a life and the Chorus wakes into it with a lead memory that *resonates* with it (always a drowned relative or counterpart of the Taken, called the **Resonance**). At dusk the Chorus steps out of the life ("goes off stage"). **The performance hardens into a Remnant**: the lead memory settles into the life as a new walk-on, with the Notes of that day (Warmth, Candor, Nerve, Curiosity, Fidelity) as its personality. That is why "whatever you do becomes permanent". The Chorus sheds one memory per life. At night it is nobody: no face the Hush will accept, visible mainly to the Awakened, to animals and to Wren.

**R14. Why the Chorus exists.** The lake's yearly spending of one memory per Taking was a quiet process. In 2024 the next memory in the queue was Wren Aldous, 9, the longest-held. She refused. The other 148 gathered around her refusal and woke as the Chorus. **Vanishings happen because understudies exist** has two layers: locally, a Harvester (Bea) has pushed a few people toward Decisions every Thin Week since 1971 so the Held can have lives. Cosmically, the Chorus needs a vacancy each morning, so the lake empties one each night. **[HARD: Bea caused some of this year's, not all.]**

**R15. The Raising.** Founders' Day. The Pageant retells the Finding. The Wish is spoken (in its cut form) by the Voice (a Ware, currently Pastor Ellis) and echoed by the town, while a nine-year-old lantern-bearer walks the lamp from the Chapel to the shore. If the Raising is skipped, the lake renews the Wish by itself and does it harshly. That is Ending 1 without a player's hands on it. **[HARD: skipping is not an ending.]**

## 3. Timeline

### Before
- **1840** Mercy is founded: *Low Mercy* in the Hollis valley and *High Mercy* ("the Rise") on the ridge, with the Chapel.
- **1938** Dr. Casimir Pell opens his practice in Low Mercy. His chair faces the window onto the river. A copy of that window, in that position, is now in Mags's office.
- **1953** The Authority surveys the valley (surveyor Walter Haldane, who draws the maps).
- **1958** Condemnation order. Buyouts begin. A custom grows up across the valley: at dusk the Stayers flash a lantern at the Rise and the Rise answers with a wave. The call across the dark is "Same time tomorrow!"
- **1959–60** Most families sell and go. **212 people refuse** (stubborn, poor, old, loyal, or just not ready). "Stayers."
- **Aug 1961** The dam is finished early. The Authority resolves to close the gates at the first surge to protect the fiscal year.

### The Night Water
- **Sat 9 Sept 1961, 6 p.m.** Clerk Edmund Pruitt signs the evacuation report: *"913 persons accounted for."* His supervisor leaned on him, and **his own sister Hattie was among the 212**. Radio station WMRC is told to hold the evacuation notice.
- **~3:50 a.m.** Remnants of a hurricane. Announcer Lowell Page disobeys and begins reading the notice on air.
- **~4:05** Gideon Brask, 19, climbs the Low Mercy church tower and rings the bell until the water reaches it. The Rise hears it and does not come down; they have been told the valley is clear. This is the Vigil's real guilt.
- **4:09** Lowell Page's last broadcast: *"…it's in the square. Fair water, Mercy. Fair water, every one of you."* (K7)
- **4:12 a.m.** Gates close. The bell stops. 212 people drown.

### Dawn
- **~6:30** On the new shoreline, Rev. Ansel Ware kneels with a lantern and prays the Wish (§6). The 701 answer: *"Let it stay."*
- **~7:00** His daughter **Eleanor "Nell" Ware**, 9, walks up the shore. She is dry. She had slept over at her best friend's house in Low Mercy. Ansel's wife **Ruth** went down for her in the dark and did not come back. **He asked for his girl and not his wife.** That is the Ware family's private shame.
- Ottoline Fisk (28) sees the child come up out of the water and does not say so for 63 years.

### After
- **1962** First Raising pageant. The Mercy Players form; the Playhouse is built from the Chapel's salvaged pews. First Taking (Oct): Silas Weatherby. Nell, 10, starts the **Prompt Book** at her father's direction.
- **1963** The pageant text is cut: *"Give me back my girl, and"* removed ("too private"). Hymn #212, "Night Water", is pulled from the hymnal.
- **1971** Bea Lindgren is Taken; Hester Voss walks in. **1979** Dolores. **1989** Roy Marsh; Silas Awakens and confronts Nell (the Waking forms). **1996** Fitz. **2008** Connie.
- **2003** Delphine Pruitt elected mayor, fourth in a line of Pruitts.
- **2009** Nell's husband Walt dies, a Remembrance with pie. **2011** Ingrid Tamm dies, no funeral. **2019** Priya Nandakumar arrives; Mags takes over the practice. **2022** The Index Club forms.

## 4. The five layers (plus the Coda)

Every lore fragment carries one **depth tag**. Players are never told which layer they're on. They notice that the earlier layers have turned over.

| Layer | Name | What the player assembles | What it re-colours |
|---|---|---|---|
| **L1** | **Surface** | Odd customs: no goodbyes, waves, clocks, Staying Days, extra plate, blue stripe, POP. 913. | Nothing yet. It's all cozy and funny. |
| **L2** | **Pattern** | Everyone who "moves away" had just made a Decision. The Index, Cal's notebook, "Visiting". | The customs stop being charming and start being *avoidance*. |
| **L3** | **History** | 1961. The reservoir, the Vigil, the falsified census, the Wish with its missing clause. | "Same time tomorrow" is a promise nobody kept. The sign counts the dead. |
| **L4** | **Others** | Walk-ons exist. Tells. Factions. The Prompt Book. Day 1 was a casting call. | The neighbours you love may be the drowned. Your own acts have been *props* in someone's stage-managing. |
| **L5** | **The Player** | The Chorus. The Remnants you left. Wren. Vanishings happen because understudies exist. | Your "personality that sticks" was the drowned slipping change into a town that forbade it. You are the town's grief, walking. |
| **Ω** | **Coda** (mastery only) | The Cut Clause. Nell is Understudy #0. *Fair water.* The Seven Keys (§9). | The ending has a fourth door. |

**Order of discovery is soft; order of *understanding* is hard.** A player can read a L4 fragment on Day 2, but it will not make sense until L2 and L3 have been assembled. Fragments are written so that the first reading gives a plausible harmless meaning and the later reading gives the true one.

## 6. The Wish

**Original** (Rev. Ansel Ware, dawn, 10 Sept 1961):
> *Lord, you have taken the valley. **Give me back my girl, and** let nothing else be taken. Let everything stay as it is.*
> — "Let it stay."

**Pageant text** (since 1963):
> *Lord, you have taken the valley. Let nothing else be taken. Let everything stay as it is.*
> — "Let it stay."

The Voice has always paused one beat after "valley." That is where the cut clause lived. **The silence is the clue.**

**The Unspeaking** (Ending 4):
> *Lord, you have kept the valley. **Let my girl go**, and let everything go as it goes.*
> — "Fair water."

## 9. The four endings: lore logic

### 1. SAME TIME TOMORROW *(Freeze the town)*
The player has the Voice speak the pageant text exactly as printed, then adds the one line the Wish never had and the lake has always wanted: **"Let nothing ever be decided again."** The Chorus is spent making a standing understudy for every living person. Nobody can be Taken because nobody can Decide. The town is happy, funny, warm, and the same, forever. The Inner Voice (Wren) stops speaking.
**Reframes:** every cozy detail. *The player becomes the thing that makes "cozy" a prison.*

### 2. LOW WATER *(Break the pact)*
Oren's gate-key and the Mayor's order open the Plug on the night of the Raising. The lake drains by dawn. All 212 Held walk out of the valley in their 1961 bodies. The 75 Taken wake on the shore. **Every walk-on dissolves**, including the ones the player made. Nell becomes a nine-year-old in the arms of her mother. The Chorus goes home as 136 people.
**Cost:** *Every NPC the player shaped is gone, and the strangers who return don't know the town.*
**Reframes:** whether the made are less real than the made-for.

### 3. STAYING DAY *(Stay in one life forever)*
The Chorus collapses into one Life. The Held lose their last chance. The pact survives. The player becomes a walk-on, with a Staying Day of their own, and years pass in a few lovely scenes. Takings continue once a year. The original of that Life stays in the Still.
**Reframes:** *the small, real life you chose came at someone's expense.*

### 4. FAIR WATER *(secret; requires the Seven Keys)*
Nell, as the last Ware and the keystone of the Wish, speaks the Unspeaking in front of a town that **notices**. Nothing is erased and nothing is drained. The lake loosens and becomes a lake. The Held are released to rest. The 75 Taken wake on the shore. The walk-ons are named in front of the town, and being known makes them people; their borrowed memories merge with their lives. Nell lets go and closes gently with Wren's hand in hers. The Chorus says goodbye, and **this is the first goodbye said aloud in Little Mercy in 63 years.**
**Reframes Day One.** The final shot recreates the opening: the lake-facing window, the cat on the sill. The lake waves back.

### The Seven Keys of Fair Water
All seven must be pinned on the Case Board *and* cross-confirmed (two independent sources each).

| Key | What | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| **K1 The Cut Clause** | Ansel's original wording | Chapel safe (Ellis, Day 13); Nell's 1962 crayon; Harlan's slip; the Voice's pause |
| **K2 The Roll** | All 212 names | Bell-tower register (names written in Ansel's hand); the census gap; the 212 strokes on the Reply Tape |

## 10. Stress test: the questions fans will ask

**Are the Taken dead?** No. They're alive, unaging, in the Still. They wake in Endings 2 and 4. Postcards arrive.

**Is the lake evil?** No. It is literal. The Wish said *everything stays as it is*, and it will not make an exception for a nice person's decision to leave.

**Why did the lake return only Nell?** Ansel asked for "my girl." Only one thing was asked. He has never forgiven himself for not asking for Ruth, and the cut clause is the Ware family's shame.

**Why didn't the town notice the first Takings?** The first generation did. They knew. They built the Playhouse and the Prompt Book as a way of staying sane, and called the vanishings "cast changes". Second and third generations grew up inside the Hush.

**Why can't walk-ons leave?** The Rim. Past it the lent memory dries out first.

**Do walk-ons have souls?** The game never says "soul". It says "person", and answers by whether the town *notices* them. Ending 4 settles the question by choice, not metaphysics.

**Did Bea cause all 13?** No. She nudged Mags, Jo and Billie. For the rest she was as surprised as anyone, and says: *"I never took anyone this fast. Something's feeding it."*

**Why do the Takes cluster before 10 Sept?** The Wish has a yearly term (R12).

**Why does the Chorus wake in Mags's house specifically?** The lead memory of Day 1 is Casimir Pell, her grandfather's, whose chair was in that window. The Chorus wakes where a memory rhymes.

**Who rang the bell at dawn?** Nobody. Nobody ever has. That sound at 4:12 is the lake clearing its throat. **[SOFT]**

**Why did Wren refuse?** She was nine. Everyone she loved was in the Held, and the next one in the queue would have walked off wearing Tobi's face. She did not want to go first.

**What does the Chorus become at the end of each ending?** 1: spent. 2: 136 people. 3: one person and 135 stranded. 4: at rest.

**Isn't 212 a lot of coincidences?** The dead number 212. Hymn #212 is "Night Water." The Reply is 212 strokes. In-world this is acknowledged: the survivors counted the dead at dawn, got 212, opened the Low Mercy hymnal, and found "Night Water" at that number. Ansel called it a sign. Nell, who was never allowed to say so, calls it a coincidence that grief made into scripture. The 212 strokes are the lake reciting the count. Gid Brask's bell is only the instrument it uses.

**Does the lake punish whistleblowers?** No (R4). The wrong theory is a deliberate red herring, and the correct answer is on the Case Board for any player who lists all thirteen Decisions side by side.

**What's the number 913 for?** It's what the Pruitt census claimed. It is also the town's sign. They are the same number because nobody wanted to count again.

**Who is the Stage Manager?** Nell. Every other guess has an alibi.

**Is there a fifth ending?** No. If a fifth is ever datamined it will be a joke.
