## The idea in four lines

- The lake is **a promise with water in it**. In 1961 a grieving pastor prayed *"Give me back my girl, and let nothing else be taken. Let everything stay as it is."* and the lake did exactly that, literally, forever.
- Anyone who **decides to change their life** is Taken at 4:12 a.m.; a walk-on built from a drowned person's memory takes their place. The town's cheerful customs are the immune system.
- **You are the Chorus**: the 149 drowned memories left over, woken because the next one in the queue, a nine-year-old called **Wren**, refused to go. Each life you wear leaves a Remnant behind. By Day 14 you are what's left.
- The **Stage Manager** who has been dressing your bedroom every morning is **Nell**, the pastor's daughter. She is not the last survivor. She is the first understudy.

## Creative calls I made that you may want to veto

1. **The player is the drowned town's collective memory**, not one person, and *sheds a piece of themselves into every role*. This is what makes "it sticks" the plot rather than a mechanic.
2. **The Day-One reframe is a casting call**: all eight Day-1 patients were booked by Nell to look at the new face. (Staged for the slice.)
3. **Nell is Understudy #0.** The founder of the ritual drowned too.
4. **The lake is not evil.** There is no villain except a sentence, and the nearest thing to one is **Bea**, who is the kindest person in town.
5. **Ending 4 requires an act of consent, not a victory**: Nell speaks the Unspeaking and dies, gently, and the whole town says *"fair water"*, the first goodbye in 63 years.
6. **Ending 2 destroys the NPCs you shaped** (they're walk-ons). That's the cost, and I've kept it.
7. **Wren is the Inner Voice**: the hint system is a funny, observant nine-year-old.
8. **The wrong theory is planted deliberately.** Several Decisions involve exposing something, so fans will think the lake silences truth-tellers. It doesn't (R4); it takes change.

## Self-critique (what I found and fixed before showing you)

| Weakness found | Fix |
|---|---|
| 6 of 13 Decisions were **disclosures**, which blurred the "decided to change their life" pattern | Rewrote as life changes (resign, retire, quit, transfer); disclosure is *how*, not *why*. Stated in R4 and as a planted red herring |
| Billie's proposal had a **logic hole** (a walk-on who says yes would be Struck) | Bea doesn't answer: *"Ask me again in the morning"* |
| **212 numerology** (dead, hymn, bell strokes) | Acknowledged in-world as grief making scripture; the 212 strokes are the lake reciting the count, not Gid's bell |
| Lives had **imprints** but thin **reactions to your other performances** | Added the Remnant-reactions table |
| **Cozy and funny** under-weighted on the page | Added voice lines throughout and a running-gags ledger where each gag carries a grief payload |
| Fragment totals were **estimated, not counted** | Counted by script (15 D, 8 V, 5 S, 7 Dr, 7 R, 9 B, 5 G, 12 Lt, 5 Rd, 14 F, 3 P = 90) |
| Smaller errors: wrong reel date (9 Sept 1961 was a Saturday), stale section refs, a muddled Roy row | Fixed |

**Still weakest, and I'd want your eye on it:**
- **Act II/III are thinner than Act I.** The Lives from Day 6 on are strong individually but share a pattern ("secret, decision, pet"). Deliverable 2 should vary their *structure* (e.g., Marcus's day is one long scene; Day 14 has no job at all).
- **The Chorus-sheds-a-piece mechanic is hard to communicate in play.** It needs a clear UI beat in Deliverable 2 (my suggestion: Nell's Notes cards, one per night).
- **Songs (5) and graffiti (5)** are the lightest fragment types. I'd like to add more in Deliverable 3.
- **Ages and pop.** Walk-ons wear the Taken's body-age at the Take, so early ones are old. Silas looks 84 with a 61-year-old's mind. I think that's a feature, but check it feels right.
