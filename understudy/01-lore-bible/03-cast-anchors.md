# UNDERSTUDY — Anchors, Ensemble & the Held

> The people who don't get taken: they watch, scheme, remember and hold the town together. Every one has an arc, a secret, a stance on change, and reactions to what the player has done as other people.
> **Spoilers.** Notes (WARMTH / CANDOR / NERVE / CURIOSITY / FIDELITY) are defined in `02-cast-lives.md`.

## A1. Eleanor "Nell" Ware-Prewitt — Playhouse director and Stage Manager, 72

**Truth:** **Understudy #0, the First Answer.** The lake gave her back to herself. She believed she had survived until her father told her, at twelve, what he'd prayed. **Her name is on the Roll, written and struck through in his hand.**
**Face:** "Auntie Nell." Gardener of the Playhouse, flinty, funny, calls people "kiddo." Her late husband Walt died in 2009. Her older brother Thomas (b. 1940, d. 2015) was Ellis's father.
**Secret:** She is the Stage Manager. She keeps the Prompt Book, ties the red ribbons, fixes the appointment books. She knows she drowned. She knows the Wish is her. And she has never forgiven herself for being the one who came back instead of Wren.
**Stance on change:** She wants it more than anyone and fears it more than anyone. Ending means she goes.

**Arc**
- **Act I.** She watches. She stages Day 1. She gives the player a mint tin with a Playhouse key inside.
- **Act II.** She tests. She sends rehearsal summonses to the Playhouse and slips "Notes" cards under the Prop Room door. They are the Notes scoring made physical.
- **Act III.** She breaks. She shows page 0 of the Prompt Book on Day 14.
- **Finale.** She speaks the Wish and the player decides what comes next.

**Endings**
1. She is the happiest she has ever been, and it is terrifying.
2. She is nine again, in her mother's arms.
3. She lives out her years as Auntie Nell, quietly proud of the player.
4. She says the Unspeaking and closes, holding Wren's hand.

**Reacts to your past performances**
- **High NERVE across lives:** *"You've got a spine, kiddo. Mags's. Good."*
- **High FIDELITY:** *"You're too good at this."* She gets more careful around the player.
- **Low CANDOR:** she lets you keep lying until it costs you.

**Tells:** She has none, which is the tell. A walk-on from 1961 has had sixty-three years of hand cream.
**Voice:** *"I'm not telling you what to do, kiddo. I'm telling you where the stairs are."*

## A2. Beatrix "Bea" Lindgren — baker, The Rising Loaf, 76

**Truth:** Walk-on (1971). Life: Beatrix Lindgren, who decided in Sept 1971 to leave for nursing school. Source: **Hester Voss, 27**, a Low Mercy baker's wife who drowned. Her three-year-old son Henry was already on the Rise. **He is Hank Moss.**
**Face:** Warm, floury, "Auntie Bea." Welcome loaves with a note: *"Hope you stay."* *"Life's short, go for it!"*
**Secret:** She is **the Gardener.** Every Thin Week since 1971 she has quietly encouraged one person toward a Decision: nursing school, a proposal, a move. The Taken are safe in the Still, and the Held deserve lives. She has never counted what the Still costs. And she raised her own son from a distance for fifty years.
**Stance on change:** Change is life. It is also, she has been avoiding noticing, murder.

**Arc**
- **Act I.** Sweet and unreadable. She says *"You're early"* to the dentist on Day 1.
- **Act II.** Billie's proposal makes her a victim of her own method. On Day 9 she recognises the player and asks them to wear Billie well.
- **Act III.** She wants the Chorus to *become lives* for the Held; she is the obstacle to Ending 4 and the reason Ending 3 is tempting.
- **Finale.** She chooses between her son and the plan.

**Endings:** (1) bakes forever. (2) dissolves; Hester walks out of the valley and sees her grown son. (3) stays, and keeps Gardening. (4) is named in front of the town and bakes a loaf for the Extra Plates.

**Reacts to your past performances**
- **High WARMTH (any life):** she gives you the first loaf of the day, still warm, and a pointed question.
- **Played Billie badly:** she freezes you out until Day 15.
- **High CURIOSITY:** *"Hon, you ask a lot of questions for someone who just got here."*

**Tells:** No cavities. Hums Hymn #212 while kneading. Cried at the 4:12 chime in 1998.
**Voice:** *"You can't unbake a loaf, hon. But you can always bake another."*

## A3. Silas Weatherby — clockmaker, 84

**Truth:** Walk-on (1962), the first. Life: Silas Weatherby, 22, who decided to join the Navy. Source: **Thaddeus Rook, 61**, Low Mercy clockmaker. The Town Hall clock's movement is Rook's, saved from the drowned church the week before.
**Face:** Wry, wears three wristwatches that show three different times, repairs everyone's clocks and never adjusts them.
**Secret:** Awakened in 1989. **Leader of the Waking.** He knows exactly what he is, has known for thirty-five years, and has not yet found the courage to ask Nell to end it.
**Stance on change:** Wants it, and has not once let himself decide it. If he decides he is Struck.
**Shop sign:** SILAS WEATHERBY & SON, with ghost lettering under the paint: **& ROOK.**

**Arc**
- **Act I.** First patient on Day 1. His slip: *"That's Casimir's crown, you know."* He gives the player a clock part.
- **Act II.** He quietly recruits, starting with Vern's Hymn.
- **Act III.** He hesitates. The player learns whether he is free to choose.
- **Finale.** In Ending 2 he meets his Source's body. In Ending 4 he is named and stays.

**Reacts to your past performances**
- **High NERVE:** he gives you the clock-pin that holds 4:12.
- **High CURIOSITY:** he teaches you Rook's trade: a clock-puzzle that hides K-grade evidence.
- **Low FIDELITY:** he says: *"Somebody's been wearing Pell wrong."*

**Tells:** Prune hands (he wears watch-gloves). Rehearsed anecdote about "my father's shop."
**Voice:** *"The trick of a clock is that it isn't telling you the time. It's keeping you company."*

## A4. Calvin "Cal" Reyes — deputy sheriff, 38

**Truth:** Human. Fourth member of the Index Club. Kept notes since 2022.
**Face:** Easy, tired, kind. Parks next to the lake each night to eat his dinner.
**Secret:** **Every morning he forgets who is gone.** The Hush works on him like anyone else. His notebook is how he remembers. On Day 1 it says: *"M. Pell. Dentist. Friend. Do not let her go."* He read it over breakfast and could not place her.
**Stance on change:** He's afraid he has already changed, and just doesn't know how.

**Arc**
- **Act I.** On Day 1 he half-recognises the player and asks: *"Do I know you?"* The only person who ever sees the Swing, unaided.
- **Act II.** His notebook is the first trusted ally, and the first unreliable one; some of its pages are copied wrong.
- **Act III.** He realises he has been writing the names of the Taken for two years.
- **Finale.** His registry is K6.

**Endings:** (1) his notebook is empty. (2) all his friends return. (3) still writing. (4) he stops writing, and remembers.

**Reacts to your past performances**
- **High CANDOR:** he trusts you with the notebook.
- **High WARMTH:** he eats dinner with you at the shore.
- **Low CURIOSITY:** *"You didn't ask me anything. That's not like Mags."*

**Voice:** *"I don't remember her, but I know I liked her. That's weird, right?"*

## A5. June Pruitt — student and street artist, 16

**Truth:** Human. Delphine's daughter. Hears Wren in dreams.
**Face:** Sarcastic, dark eyeliner, backpack full of stencils.
**Secret:** She is the graffiti artist whose **FAIR WATER** stencil appears on the dam, the bus shelter and the library wall, painted over by Hank within hours. She doesn't know what it means. *"It came to me."*
**Stance on change:** Craves it, and thinks she's the only one.

**Arc**
- **Act I.** Hostile and funny. Her stencil is seen but unexplained.
- **Act II.** She finds her mother's vault key.
- **Act III.** She paints the K7 phrase on the Playhouse in the dark.
- **Finale.** Her stencil is the town's first public "goodbye."

**Endings:** (1) her stencils are gone. (2) her mother's family reunites with the drowned. (3) she leaves for art school and does not decide. (4) she paints the phrase on the sign: POP. 913 → FAIR WATER.

**Reacts to your past performances**
- **High NERVE:** she shows you her studio (a shed).
- **High WARMTH:** she lets you see the notebooks.
- **Played Delphine badly:** *"I know you're not her. I just don't know what you are."*

**Voice:** *"It's not graffiti if it's true."*

## A6. Adaeze Okafor — clinic nurse, 38

**Truth:** Human. Tobi's mother.
**Face:** Brisk, decisive, loves her son out loud.
**Secret:** For twelve years she has been signing death certificates as *"heart failure"* for people whose lungs were full of lake water. *It didn't fit on the form.* Edmund Pruitt's heir in the nursing wing: **every generation has its falsifier.**
**Stance on change:** Change is danger; she is protective to the point of denial.

**Arc**
- **Act I.** Day 3 is Tobi's day. She is the person standing between you and the door.
- **Act II.** She starts to notice the walk-on Tobi.
- **Act III.** Marcus's lock-box shows her the nine.
- **Finale.** She either signs the truth or doesn't.

**Endings:** (1) no more drownings in bed. (2) strangers at the clinic. (3) she keeps the certificates. (4) she files them all, amended.

**Reacts to your past performances**
- **Played Tobi with WARMTH:** she hugs the walk-on Tobi longer.
- **High CANDOR:** she tells you about the certificates.
- **Low FIDELITY as Tobi:** *"My son doesn't talk like that."*

**Voice:** *"I'm a nurse. I don't have to understand it. I have to keep him alive."*

## A7. Daniel Marsh — marina owner, 41

**Truth:** Human. Tobi's father; Jo's boss.
**Face:** Easy, big laugh, always smells of diesel.
**Secret:** He wants to leave and has an unsigned contract in his desk. **He has kept himself undecided for six years on purpose.** In 1989, when he was six, his father Roy came back from the hardware store "different, gentler." Daniel didn't need it explained.
**Stance on change:** Indecision as survival. He's mastered not deciding.

**Arc**
- **Act I.** He's Tobi's dad and Jo's boss, a kind man who can't say anything.
- **Act II.** His son comes back strange.
- **Act III.** He notices he's being brave for the first time.
- **Finale.** In Ending 4 he signs, and he is not Taken.

**Endings:** (1) forever undecided. (2) Roy's real wife returns. (3) the marina stays. (4) he signs, finally.

**Reacts to your past performances**
- **High WARMTH (as Tobi):** he sits on the end of the bed.
- **Played Jo with NERVE:** *"Jo's going to go. I can tell."* A trade of complicity.
- **High FIDELITY:** he doesn't notice.

**Voice:** *"It's not that I can't decide. It's that I'm very good at being about to."*

## A8. Lucinda "Lux" Brask — bar manager, lead guitar, 35

**Truth:** Human. Jo's older sister.
**Face:** Loud, funny, tenderly furious.
**Secret:** She told Jo to go and meant the opposite. She also counted the Reply Tape: **212**.
**Stance on change:** Needs the band to stay the same. Cannot say so.

**Arc**
- **Act I.** Toothache on Day 1 in Mags's chair; announces Jo is "leaving Friday."
- **Act II.** After Jo's Take she says he "never wanted to go" (the Hush). The notes in her phone say otherwise.
- **Act III.** She finds the Tape.
- **Finale.** She plays the Reply at the Raising.

**Endings:** (1) forever a trio. (2) Gid Brask, 19, walks out of the valley in his drumming jacket, and it is the first time she's seen him. (3) she keeps the band. (4) she plays the 212 strokes.

**Reacts to your past performances**
- **High WARMTH as Jo:** she hugs the walk-on Jo.
- **High NERVE as Jo:** *"Don't leave."* (The Remnant stayed.)
- **Low CANDOR:** she catches the lie.

**Voice:** *"I'm not mad at him for going. I'm mad at him for going and being right."*

## A9. Harlan Pruitt — former mayor, 86

**Truth:** Human; resident of Lakeview Rest. Delphine's father. In 1961, at 23, he was a clerk in the Authority office and watched his father sign.
**Face:** Courtly, absent, calls staff "Edmund" or "Hattie."
**Secret:** *"I signed the sheet."* He didn't sign. He **held the pen**. The Hush is thin on him, and he says things.
**Stance on change:** Wants forgiveness more than anything and cannot ask.

**Arc**
- **Act I.** Voicemails and a visit.
- **Act II.** A lucid minute with Delphine.
- **Act III.** The Roll in the hymnal.
- **Finale.** He speaks at the Raising, or is spoken for.

**Endings:** (1) forgets again. (2) sees Hattie, his aunt, stand in the doorway. (3) asks for pie. (4) says her name aloud.

**Reacts to your past performances**
- **High WARMTH:** he thinks you are Hattie and tells you everything.
- **Played Delphine with CANDOR:** he recognises her voice.
- **Low FIDELITY:** *"You're not my daughter."*

**Voice:** *"The pen was green. I remember because it leaked."*

## A10. Henry "Hank" Moss — hardware store owner, 66

**Truth:** Human. **Bea's son.** Raised by his aunt after the flood; doesn't know Bea is his mother's memory.
**Face:** Gruff, loud, loves tradition, heckles the mayor.
**Secret:** He repaints the graffiti at dawn and keeps the Blue Line fresh. He thinks it's civic duty. He suspects it's penance for something he can't name. He remembers cinnamon.
**Stance on change:** Defender of Mercy as it is; secretly just wants his mother.

**Arc**
- **Act I.** Day 1's second patient; reports to Dolores. Hecklers.
- **Act II.** He finds one of June's stencils under his own paint.
- **Act III.** He sees Bea's hands.
- **Finale.** He must choose between the town he repaints and the mother he can't have.

**Endings:** (1) paints forever. (2) his mother walks out of the valley at 27. (3) keeps painting. (4) leaves the stencil.

**Reacts to your past performances**
- **High WARMTH as anyone:** he gives you a free can of paint.
- **High NERVE as Delphine:** he heckles harder.
- **High CURIOSITY:** *"You ask like a Pruitt."*

**Voice:** *"It's a stripe. It's a nice stripe. Don't make it a thing."*

## A11. Dolores Vance — organist, 69

**Truth:** Walk-on (1979). Life: Dolores Vance, 24. Source: **Millie Vance, 52**, Low Mercy Chapel's organist, still playing as the water came into the church.
**Face:** Gracious, severe, in the choir's front row.
**Secret:** **Leader of the Holdfast.** She removed Hymn #212 from the hymnal in 1963, as the Source, and sincerely believes the lake gave them mercy. She is terrified that if the pact goes the water comes back. She also quietly corrects Cal's notebook in a second hand (matching her choir attendance sheets) whenever he writes a name too plainly.
**Stance on change:** Stasis as grace.

**Arc**
- **Act I.** A cold stare from the choir loft.
- **Act II.** Sabotages the Hymn, the Waking and Mags's Match List.
- **Act III.** Confronts the player with the thing she's most afraid of.
- **Finale.** She plays the Raising's organ. What she plays decides it.

**Endings:** (1) plays perfectly. (2) dissolves. (3) plays on. (4) plays the Hymn.

**Reacts to your past performances**
- **High NERVE:** she begins to treat you as an enemy.
- **High FIDELITY:** *"You have such lovely manners."*
- **Played Vern:** she knows you aired the Hymn and doesn't speak of it.

**Tells:** Hums the first bar of #212 when she thinks she's alone. Rehearsed story of "my mother, God rest her."
**Voice:** *"I didn't take anything from anyone. I kept what was left."*

## A12. Fitzgerald "Fitz" Merriman — bartender, The Low Water, 48

**Truth:** Walk-on (1996). Life: Fitzgerald Merriman, 20. Source: **Ambrose Quill, 40**, tavern keeper.
**Face:** Garrulous. The best story in town, told again, differently.
**Secret:** Every story is half true. He lies about his own life because he has two and cannot tell them apart.
**Stance on change:** A Hider. He wants it to be Thursday forever.

**Arc**
- **Act I.** Jo's gig venue. Comic relief with edges.
- **Act II.** He tells tall tales that happen to be true.
- **Act III.** He admits he's Quill.
- **Finale.** He pours for the Extra Plates.

**Endings:** (1) bartends forever. (2) Quill, 40, in the valley with a full glass. (3) keeps his stool. (4) tells the true story at the bar.

**Reacts to your past performances**
- **High WARMTH:** free beer and a different, truer story.
- **High CURIOSITY:** he tells the lie that fits.
- **Played Jo:** he expects a set list.

**Tells:** Damp cuff, always. Never tells the same story twice, which is itself a tell: *a rehearsed anecdote that's never rehearsed twice.*
**Voice:** *"That's a true story. Well. It happened to somebody."*

## A13. Wren Aldous — the Held, 9 *(the Inner Voice)*

**Truth:** Drowned 1961. The longest-held. The Chorus's keel. The one who refused. **The Inner Voice** the player hears as their own hints is hers. It sounds like a funny nine-year-old.
**Face:** Cheeky, clear, impatient, adult-adjacent.
**Secret:** The sleepover was her idea, and she has been sorry for sixty-three years. Nell has been sorry for the opposite reason. Her dog **Pickle** went down with her and came up alone. He is the Lake Dog.
**Stance on change:** She isn't afraid of it. She's afraid of being first.

**Arc**
- **Act I.** Commentary, jokes, small corrections.
- **Act II.** She speaks to Tobi; she speaks to the dog.
- **Act III.** Day 14: she speaks directly.
- **Finale.** In Ending 4 she and Nell hold hands.

**Endings:** (1) silenced. (2) walks out of the valley in a nightgown. (3) keeps speaking, quieter. (4) says "Fair water" and goes.

**Reacts to your past performances:** she keeps score on the Notes and teases accordingly. *High WARMTH: "Nice." Low NERVE: "That was a bit squeaky."*

**Voice:** *"Okay. New mouth. Let's not eat anything weird."*
